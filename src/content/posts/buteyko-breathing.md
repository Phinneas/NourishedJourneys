<p>Buteyko breathing is a therapeutic method developed by Dr. Konstantin Buteyko in the 1950s. It focuses on reducing chronic hyperventilation, or over-breathing, which is linked to asthma, anxiety, snoring, and poor sleep.</p><p>The core principle is counterintuitive: breathing less, not more, can improve oxygen delivery to tissues. When you over-breathe, you exhale too much carbon dioxide. This causes blood vessels to constrict and reduces oxygen release from hemoglobin.</p><h2 id="the-buteyko-method-basics">The Buteyko Method Basics</h2><p>Buteyko breathing has two core exercises:</p><h3 id="1-nasal-breathing">1. Nasal Breathing</h3><p>Breathe through your nose at all times, including during sleep and exercise. Nasal breathing filters, warms, and humidifies air. It also naturally slows breathing rate and increases carbon dioxide levels.</p><h3 id="2-controlled-pause">2. Controlled Pause</h3><p>Follow these steps:</p><ul><li><strong>Step 1:</strong> Sit upright and breathe normally through your nose for 1 minute.</li><li><strong>Step 2:</strong> After a normal exhale, pinch your nose closed and hold your breath.</li><li><strong>Step 3:</strong> Time how many seconds pass before you feel the first urge to breathe. Do not hold until you are desperate.</li><li><strong>Step 4:</strong> Release your nose and resume nasal breathing. Your first breath should be calm and controlled, not gasping.</li></ul><p>A healthy control pause is 40-60 seconds. Below 20 seconds indicates significant over-breathing.</p><h2 id="the-reduced-breathing-exercise">The Reduced Breathing Exercise</h2><p>Once you know your control pause, practice reduced breathing:</p><ul><li><strong>Step 1:</strong> Sit comfortably and breathe normally through your nose for 1 minute.</li><li><strong>Step 2:</strong> Take a slightly smaller inhale than normal, then relax and let a passive exhale happen.</li><li><strong>Step 3:</strong> Continue taking gentle, shallow breaths for 3-5 minutes. You should feel a slight air hunger, but not distress.</li><li><strong>Step 4:</strong> Return to normal breathing for 1 minute.</li></ul><h2 id="safety-and-contraindications">Safety and Contraindications</h2><p>Do not practice breath-holding if you are pregnant, have uncontrolled high blood pressure, or cardiovascular disease. Stop if you feel dizzy, nauseous, or anxious. Consult a healthcare provider before using Buteyko for asthma management.</p><h2 id="expected-benefits">Expected Benefits</h2><p>With consistent practice, many people experience reduced asthma symptoms, better sleep, less anxiety, and improved athletic endurance. Results typically appear within 2-4 weeks of daily practice.</p>
