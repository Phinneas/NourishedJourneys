<p>Coherent breathing, also called resonant breathing, is the practice of breathing at a rate of about 5 to 6 breaths per minute. At this pace, your heart rate variability reaches its optimal state, creating a powerful synchronization between your heart, breath, and nervous system.</p><p>This is not just a relaxation technique. Research shows that resonant breathing improves emotional regulation, cognitive performance, and cardiovascular health. It is used clinically for PTSD, depression, and hypertension.</p><h2 id="how-to-practice-coherent-breathing">How to Practice Coherent Breathing</h2><p>The standard protocol is 5 breaths per minute, which means:</p><ul><li><strong>Inhale:</strong> 5-6 seconds</li><li><strong>Exhale:</strong> 5-6 seconds</li><li><strong>Total cycle:</strong> 10-12 seconds</li></ul><p>Follow these steps:</p><ul><li><strong>Step 1:</strong> Sit or lie comfortably with your spine straight.</li><li><strong>Step 2:</strong> Breathe in through your nose for a slow count of 5.</li><li><strong>Step 3:</strong> Breathe out through your nose for a slow count of 5.</li><li><strong>Step 4:</strong> Continue for 10-20 minutes. Use a timer or metronome if needed.</li></ul><h2 id="the-science-of-resonance">The Science of Resonance</h2><p>Your cardiovascular system has a natural resonance frequency around 0.1 Hz, or 6 breaths per minute. At this rate, the oscillations of your heart rate, blood pressure, and breathing align in phase.</p><p>This resonance amplifies heart rate variability, which is a key marker of autonomic nervous system health. Higher HRV is associated with better stress resilience, emotional regulation, and overall health.</p><h2 id="variations">Variations</h2><p>If 5 seconds feels too fast or too slow, adjust within the 4-7 second range for each phase. The key is finding the rate that feels effortless and produces a sense of calm alertness.</p><p>Some practitioners prefer a slightly longer exhale, such as 5 seconds in and 6 seconds out. If coherent breathing feels awkward or your breath pattern seems "off," our guide on <a href="/deep-belly-breathing-feels-off/">why deep belly breathing feels off</a> covers common mechanics mismatches. Experiment to find your personal resonance frequency.</p><h2 id="when-to-practice">When to Practice</h2><p>Practice coherent breathing for 10-20 minutes daily, ideally at the same time. Morning sessions set a calm tone for the day. Evening sessions improve sleep quality. You can also use it before stressful events or difficult conversations.</p>
